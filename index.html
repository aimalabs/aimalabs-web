The problem · 01

The gold-standard test most patients never receive.

The peripheral blood smear is the gold standard supporting the diagnosis of leukaemias, anaemias and more. But manual review is slow, depends on scarce specialists, and the machines that automate it cost more than most labs can justify.

86%
of the world’s 5 billion blood counts a year never reach a microscope — 4.3 billion counts where nobody looks at the cells.
15%
of leukaemia patients remain undiagnosed even when the CBC is abnormal — because the smear that would have found them was never reviewed (Leukaemia UK).
10–20 min
per smear for manual review — and results vary observer to observer, when a morphologist is on site at all.
£100k+
capital outlay for existing digital-morphology systems, pricing out most mid-sized labs worldwide.
The problem · 02

The test that finds what the count misses.

A blood count is automated and cheap — it answers how many cells? The smear is one glass slide read under a microscope, and it answers how do they look? That is where the diagnosis actually lives.

BLOOD COUNT (CBC)

“How many cells?”

Automated · 5 billion a year. Fast and cheap — but blind to what the cells look like.

BLOOD SMEAR

“How do they look?”

Manual · 10–20 minutes of expert time. Shape, size and structure of every cell — and 86% of the time it simply is not done.
